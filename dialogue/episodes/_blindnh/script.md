# "What happens when I hit send?" (campfire, Leo + Walter, ~45 s final, 136 words, voice at 1.5x)

Premise: Leo has just texted Maya, a friend who lives in London, and asks where the message actually goes. Walter, his grandfather, warm and a little amused, walks it through in plain words. Both stay seated by the fire the whole time. The phone exists only in words and in the MG.
Payload: a text hops from your phone by radio to the nearest tower, then runs as light through cables (under the sea for far-away friends), through a company's computers, to the other phone, in well under a second.
Human question: "Is my message really going 'through the air' to her?" Turn: it is mostly glass cables on the sea floor. Landing: the network is fast; the slow part is the person.

LEO (curious): Grandpa, when I text Maya in London, where does it go?
WALTER (warm): First, to the nearest phone tower.
LEO (surprised): Through the air?
WALTER (amused): Radio waves. Your phone shouts, and the tower listens.
LEO (curious): Then the tower sends it to her?
WALTER (matter-of-fact): Not yet. Into cables. Under the street, then under the sea.
LEO (confused): Under the sea? I thought texts used satellites.
WALTER (firm, light): Mostly cables. Ninety-nine percent, between continents.
LEO (shocked): So my text goes swimming?
WALTER (amused): As light, inside glass threads.
LEO (curious): Okay. And then?
WALTER (warm): A company's computers. Like the building with your photos. They find Maya's phone and pass it on.
LEO (thinking): Like passing a note across the classroom?
WALTER (pleased): Exactly. Hand to hand, right into her pocket.
LEO (curious): How long does all that take? A minute?
WALTER (firm, light): Less than a second.
LEO (grinning): Then why does Maya take an hour to reply?
WALTER (dry, smiling): That part's Maya.

#end

Delivery notes (voice): normal conversational pace (the track is stretched 1.5x after). Leo: curious, then surprised, playful, a grin on the last question. Walter: warm, smiling in the voice, falls to a low finish; only the last line is dry and flat.
Seated: no actions at all. Nobody takes out a phone; the phone, tower, cables and computers are words + MG only.
Gestures (seated only, picture plan in PLAN.md): Walter open palm "tower", palm down "sea", two-hand open "Hand to hand"; Leo palm up "air", hand lift "swimming", hand to chest "I thought".

## Craft pass (draft -> 3 critics -> one rewrite)
- Rules: hook = name + worry, answered < 3 s; Leo is the proxy (every line a question / echo / wrong conclusion, <= 11 words); Walter 2-9 word sentences; one plain number (99 %), Leo restates it ("goes swimming?"), one analogy (passing a note in class); wrong number then correction ("A minute?" / "Less than a second."); callback to the cloud episode (the building with your photos); dry button ("That part's Maya."), no moral. One "not yet" (no "Not X, Y" rhythm). No rule-of-three lists.
- Blind test (names stripped, 10 random lines): 10/10 guessable: every Leo line is a question, every Walter line a short declarative.
- Critic 1 (table read, hook 7/10): "where does it actually go" was soft -> made concrete with "Maya in London" (gives the undersea leg a reason). Line 12 was one 20-word sentence -> split into three short sentences for 1.5x.
- Critic 2 (voice/blind): Walter's "Not yet. It drops into cables..." too explainy -> "Not yet. Into cables."
- Critic 3 (fact/logic): "texts use satellites" is Leo's wrong belief, Walter corrects to "mostly cables" (true: satellites carry a tiny share). "Under the street" = tower backhaul is mostly fibre (some microwave links) -> kept, "mostly" covers it. The 99 % figure is for intercontinental traffic, so the line says "between continents".
