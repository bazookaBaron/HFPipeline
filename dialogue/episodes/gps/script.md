# "How does my phone know where I am?" (campfire, Leo + Walter, ~50 s final, voice at 1.5x)

Premise: Leo's phone dot says he is at the fire, and he cannot let it go. Walter, his grandfather, walks him through GPS in plain words. Both stay seated by the fire the whole time. The phone exists only in words and in the MG.
Payload: satellites broadcast the time; the phone measures how late each signal arrives, late x speed of light = distance; four such distances (spheres) cross in one point = you; the clocks on the satellites even need Einstein's correction (~38 microseconds a day); in cities maps and wifi help.
Human question: "Is something up there watching me?" Turn: the satellites don't know you exist, they only say the time. Landing: the phone always knows; the people get lost.

LEO (curious) [CU]: Grandpa, my phone says I'm at the fire. How does it know?
WALTER (warm) [MS]: Satellites. Twenty thousand kilometres up. They only say the time.
LEO (surprised) [OTS]: Just the time? That's all?
WALTER (amused) [MS]: Over and over. Your phone hears it a tiny bit late.
LEO (curious) [OTS]: Late by how much?
WALTER (matter-of-fact) [MS]: Enough to measure. Late times the speed of light is distance.
LEO (thinking) [OTS]: So one satellite says I'm this far from it.
WALTER (firm, light) [MS]: A ball of possible places. Four satellites, four balls. They cross in one spot. That's you.
LEO (confused) [OTS]: Why four? Three would cross.
WALTER (warm) [2S]: Your phone's clock is cheap. The fourth one fixes it.
LEO (shocked) [CU]: Light is fast. One tiny mistake and I'm in the ocean?
WALTER (matter-of-fact) [MS]: A millionth of a second is three hundred metres. So the satellites carry atomic clocks, and Einstein corrects them. Thirty-eight microseconds a day.
LEO (grinning) [OTS]: Einstein works for my phone?
WALTER (amused) [MS]: Skip him, and you drift ten kilometres a day.
LEO (curious) [OTS]: And in the city, between buildings?
WALTER (pleased) [MS]: Maps and wifi names fill the gaps.
LEO (grinning) [CU]: Okay, but then why were we lost last summer?
WALTER (dry, smiling) [CU]: The phone knew exactly where we were. You were the lost one.

#end

Delivery notes (voice): normal conversational pace (the track is stretched 1.5x after). Leo: curious, surprised, playful, a grin on the last question. Walter: warm, smiling in the voice, falls to a low finish; only the last line is dry and flat.
Seated: no actions at all. Nobody takes out a phone; the phone, satellites, spheres and clocks are words + MG only.
