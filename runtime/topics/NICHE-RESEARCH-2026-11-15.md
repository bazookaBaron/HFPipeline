# Niche research for Partition Lines and Powder Keg (2026-11-15)

**Question:** Which two different niches should Partition Lines and Powder Keg each take? Each one should be not yet saturated, able to go viral, high-potential, high-RPM, and a fit for our white miniature-world documentary style.

**Short answer:**

- **Powder Keg → Corporate collapses and white-collar fraud.** "How a company blew up": rooms where decisions were made, documents, money flows. This is the highest-paying niche we can credibly make.
- **Partition Lines → Geo-economics of borders, chokepoints and trade routes.** "The lines on the map that move money": straits, ports, pipelines, sanctions, borders, resources. This niche has the strongest 2026 demand signal and is the best fit for our maps-plus-miniature-cities look.

The two differ in audience (investors and business readers versus geography and world-affairs viewers), advertisers (finance/B2B versus education/general), dominant visual (rooms and documents versus maps and ports), and story shape (a rise-and-fall arc versus a system explainer). That makes the test meaningful.

> **Every RPM/CPM figure below is a published range, not a promise.** RPM is only measured inside a channel's own YouTube Studio. Third-party tables disagree by 2–5x. The one dataset built from real Studio numbers (AIR, 300 channels) has tiny samples for some niches. Where a figure is CPM (what advertisers pay), the creator's RPM is roughly 40–55% of it, because YouTube keeps 45% and not every view carries an ad ([OutlierKit RPM benchmarks](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-rpm-benchmarks/), [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)).

**How to read this:**

1. Finance, insurance and legal sit at the top of every CPM table. Business sits just under them. Education is the best measured median.
2. News & Politics is low in the only measured dataset. Geopolitics framed as news earns like news. Framed as economics or education, it can earn like education or business. That is why we frame Partition Lines as geo-economics, not war news.
3. Shorts pay roughly 1–3% of long-form per view. Shorts are for discovery, not income.

## 2. Ranked shortlist (6 candidates)

Scoring keys:

- **Saturation:** how crowded the top of YouTube is with strong faceless or semi-faceless channels.
- **Viral potential:** the topic supply in 2026 and the track record of breakout videos.
- **Style fit (1–10):** how much our miniature rooms, cities, maps, documents and red focus colour add that stock footage cannot.
- **Ad-safety:** risk of limited ads under YouTube's [advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278).

### #1 Corporate collapses and white-collar fraud ("how it blew up")

- **RPM:** business $6–16 RPM and finance $8–20 RPM ([1of10](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rpm-by-niche/)). Business documentary CPM is about $10–25 ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-business-documentary-channels/)). The measured Business & Finance median is only $2.01, from 3 channels ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)), with Q4 about +60%. **Realistic planning band: $4–12 RPM**, with a ceiling above that if the audience is US/UK-heavy.
- **Saturation: medium-high at the top, with clear gaps.** ColdFusion has 5.2M, Coffeezilla about 4.7M ([SpeakRJ, Jul 2026](https://www.speakrj.com/audit/report/Coffeezilla/youtube)), MagnatesMedia 1.9M, Company Man 1.8M, How Money Works 1.7M, Business Casual 1.1M, Logically Answered 912K, Modern MBA 801K and Wall Street Millennial 366K ([OutlierKit teardown](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-business-documentary-channels/)). OutlierKit notes it is normal to be the 2nd–4th channel on a story, because audiences rewatch famous collapses told differently. Gaps:
  - Fresh 2026 collapses, since nobody has covered them yet.
  - Non-US scandals.
  - A reconstructed-room format. Every competitor uses archive or stock footage, slides or 2D animation; nobody rebuilds the boardroom.
  - Small fraud channels are thin (The Deception Bureau's best video had 6.8K views; [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/thedeceptionbureau)).
- **Viral potential: high.** US corporate bankruptcies hit a 15–16-year high: 785 large filings in 2025, the most since 2010, and 372 in H1 2026 ([S&P Global MI, Jan 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/1/us-corporate-bankruptcy-filings-accelerate-further-in-december-2025-96788192), [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/us-corporate-bankruptcies-hover-16-year-high-sp/825121)). The story supply is very large and constantly refreshed. Business Casual and Modern MBA average roughly as many views per video as they have subscribers, a sign the format travels beyond subscribers.
- **Style fit: 9/10.** The beats this niche needs are:
  - the room where the decision was made
  - the memo
  - the auditor's office
  - the empty trading floor
  - the money flow drawn as a red line through a white miniature city
  - one red figure (the founder) among white mannequins

  All of these are native to our look. Real executives appear only on small credited photo cards, which follows our no-AI-real-faces rule.
- **Ad-safety: high.** The topics are non-violent. The main risk is defamation (alleged fraud by living people), not ad suitability. Use "charged with", "convicted" and "according to the SEC filing".
- **Example videos:**
  1. *The Spreadsheet That Sank a $40 Billion Company*
  2. *Inside the Room Where Wirecard Invented €1.9 Billion*
  3. *Why 785 Big US Companies Went Bankrupt in One Year*
  4. *The Auditor Who Signed Everything: How One Firm Missed It All*
  5. *How a Grocery Delivery Unicorn Burned $5 Billion in 3 Years*
- **Competitors to study:** MagnatesMedia (1.9M), ColdFusion (5.2M), Coffeezilla (about 4.7M), Company Man (1.8M), Modern MBA (801K), Business Casual (1.1M), Wall Street Millennial (366K).

### #2 Geo-economics of borders, chokepoints and trade routes ("the lines that move money")

- **RPM:** depends on framing.
  - As education or economics: $3–7 RPM ([1of10](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rpm-by-niche/)), with a measured Education & Science median of $10.22 ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)).
  - As news or politics: about $2.60 measured median ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)), or $5–13 for US audiences ([OutlierKit news](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-news-channel-rpm/)).

  **Realistic planning band: $3–9 RPM.** Trade, shipping, commodities and sanctions topics pull finance and B2B advertisers toward the top of that band. War-heavy topics pull it down.
- **Saturation: medium.** Johnny Harris has about 7.8M ([SpeakRJ, Jul 2026](https://www.speakrj.com/audit/report/UCmGSJVG3mCRXVOP4yZrU1Dw/youtube)). Others: Peter Zeihan 957K, CaspianReport about 520K ([Feedspot](https://videos.feedspot.com/geopolitics_youtube_channels/)), Geopolitical Economy Report about 752K ([Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/geopoliticaleconomyreport)), Good Times Bad Times 356K ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/geopoliticsinconflict)), plus RealLifeLore and Wendover (large; counts not verified in this pass). The gap is the economic-mechanism explainer: what physically happens to a tanker, a port or a price when a line on the map changes, shown step by step in miniature. Most competitors are presenter-led (Harris, Zeihan) or talking-map news (Caspian).
- **Viral potential: very high right now.** Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz after US–Israeli strikes in February 2026. *What's Going On With Shipping?* grew from 137 subscribers (2021) to more than 700K, with several million views a month. Its audience turned out to want niche topics like marine insurance and shipping channels ([CJR, Aug 2026](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/the-shipping-news-stars-sal-mercogliano-strait-of-hormuz-crisis-commercial-maritime-moment-iran-oman-houthi-ever-given-bab-al-mandab.php)). The topic supply is permanent: tariffs, sanctions, rare earths, Panama/Suez/Malacca, the Arctic route, undersea cables, border disputes.
- **Style fit: 10/10.** Every concept our style has can be used in each video:
  - paper maps that fold into miniature ports
  - a red tanker crossing a white sea
  - a customs post or border fence in tilt-shift
  - a negotiating room with a table and mannequins
  - a data card of the price spike
- **Ad-safety: medium.** Non-graphic educational discussion of war is eligible for full ads. Raw conflict footage without context is limited. Graphic war imagery gets no ads. Content that "exploits, dismisses or condones" the Ukraine war is ineligible ([YouTube AFG](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278)). Our no-real-footage, no-gore format sits on the safe side by default. Keep titles and thumbnails free of explosions and casualties.
- **Example videos:**
  1. *The 21-Mile Line That Controls a Fifth of the World's Oil*
  2. *Why a Ship in Hormuz Now Costs $1 Million to Insure*
  3. *The Border Nobody Can Draw: Why the Arctic Is Being Divided Right Now*
  4. *How One Chinese Export Ban Can Stop a Car Factory in Ohio*
  5. *The Undersea Cable Map: Who Can Cut the Internet, and Where*
- **Competitors to study:** Johnny Harris (about 7.8M), RealLifeLore, Wendover Productions, CaspianReport (about 520K), Peter Zeihan (957K), What's Going On With Shipping? (about 700K+), Geopolitical Economy Report (about 752K).

### #3 Megaprojects, cities and real-estate economics ("why this city/project went wrong")

- **RPM:** real estate $5–12 RPM ($12–30 CPM; [1of10](https://www.1of10.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rpm-by-niche/)). Virlo gives $15–30 CPM ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-rpm-benchmarks/)). Mortgage and property advertisers pay premium CPMs.
- **Saturation: low-medium.** The B1M is the giant at about 4.0M ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@theb1m/)). Second-tier faceless competition is thin.
- **Viral potential: medium-high.** Failed megacities (NEOM/The Line), ghost cities, housing crises, stadium white elephants and HS2 all reliably interest broad audiences.
- **Style fit: 10/10.** Tilt-shift miniature cities are literally the visual language of urbanism.
- **Ad-safety: very high.**
- **Example videos:**
  1. *The Line: What's Actually Been Built in the Desert*
  2. *Why China Built Cities for Millions and Nobody Came*
  3. *The £100 Billion Train Line That Got Shorter Every Year*
  4. *Why Your Rent Is Set by an Algorithm*
  5. *The Stadium a City Is Still Paying For 30 Years Later*
- **Competitors:** The B1M (about 4.0M), Wendover Productions, Practical Engineering (about 4.8M, infrastructure).
- **Why not top 2:** neither channel name fits it well, and it overlaps with Partition Lines' maps. It is the best backup niche or a third-channel candidate.

- **RPM:** Transport measured median $5.69 (P25–P75 $3.17–9.81, 7 channels). Education and engineering is up to $10.22 median ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)). **Planning band: $4–9 RPM.**

Plainly Difficult is not verified. Many small aviation-disaster channels have under 35K. ## 3. Formats and trends working now (2025–2026)

- **Armchair documentary in a recognisable house style is winning.** fern (3D low-poly investigations) has 5.58M subscribers and about 4.8M average views per video, and "many new documentary channels copy" its consistent look. LEMMiNO grew to 5.92M with only 171 research-heavy videos ([TaleTok, Oct 2026](https://taletok.io/blog/best-faceless-youtube-channels/)). The lesson for us: one consistent, ownable visual identity plus deep research beats volume. Our white-miniature look is a fern-class signature if we keep it consistent.
- **The fastest-growing faceless format of 2026 is stickman explainers of 10–15 minutes.** Mack reached 89.5K in about 5 months with 103 videos. Axen reached 63.9K with 24 longer videos, averaging about 266K per upload. Fewer, longer, better videos outperformed volume ([TaleTok](https://taletok.io/blog/best-faceless-youtube-channels/)).
- **Education is the only niche high in both growth (1.30% a month) and pay ($10.22 median RPM)** ([AIR/TubeAI](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/whos-growing-fastest-on-youtube-and-which-niches-are-hot-in-2025)). Both our niches should be framed as explainers ("how it worked"), not opinion or news.
- **TV screens favour long, chaptered documentaries.** More than 44% of US YouTube watch time is on connected TVs. About a quarter of YouTube users watch documentaries ([TV Technology / Ampere](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/nearly-four-in-ten-youtube-users-watch-tv-and-film-content), [search summary](https://www.emarketer.com/content/youtube-ctv-growth-pricing-edge-boost-2026-streaming-outlook)). This supports 12–18-minute films with chapters, and simple thumbnails readable from a sofa ([OutlierKit TV guide](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-tv-viewers-creator-strategy/)).
- **Story shapes that keep landing:**
  - "How X collapsed" or "rise and fall" (MagnatesMedia, Company Man)
  - "the minute-by-minute" (disasters, heists)
  - "the line on the map" (Harris, RealLifeLore)
  - "why it costs $X now" (How Money Works)

## 4. What our miniature-world style does uniquely well

1. **Rooms where decisions were made.** Competitors cannot film inside a boardroom in 2019 or a ministry at 3 a.m. We can build it, freeze it and push in. Most business documentaries fall back on stock B-roll ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-business-documentary-channels/)).
2. **Maps that become places.** A paper map folds up into a tilt-shift port or border town. Map-talk channels stay flat.
3. **Red as the single focus colour.** One red founder, one red tanker, one red line of money: instant visual grammar and an ownable thumbnail.
4. **Faceless mannequins solve two problems.** No AI likeness of real people (policy and ethics), and no gore. Tragedy is shown as frozen, non-graphic scenes, which keeps videos ad-eligible.
5. **Documents as evidence.** Credited real photo and document cards (SEC filings, court exhibits, shipping notices) add the "this is real" signal that AI-slop channels lack, and support the originality case under YPP.

**Weak spots:**

- Fast-moving news, because each film takes hours to build.
- Emotional human testimony, which has no faces.
- Highly visual real events, where viewers expect the footage.

Choose topics where the mechanism matters more than the footage.

## 5. Faceless and AI-voice monetisation risk, and what to do

**Policy facts:**

- On **15 July 2025** YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to **"inauthentic content"**. It called this a clarification, not a new ban ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/), [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)).
- The current YPP text ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)) says these are **not allowed**:
  - content that "looks like it's made with a template"
  - videos with minimal variation
  - "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative"
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates"
- The same page says these are **allowed**:
  - AI used to realise "your own creative vision"
  - series with a shared format where "each video has its own storyline, focus or concept"
  - a shared intro and outro
- **Reused content** covers material made mostly of readings of text you did not create, such as news articles. It applies channel-wide: if YouTube cannot tell you made the content, the whole channel can lose monetisation.
- **AI disclosure:** a generic AI voice over truthful visuals does not by itself need the "altered or synthetic" label. A synthetic version of a **real person's** voice does, and so do photorealistic synthetic scenes ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content), [AIR disclosure guide](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will)). Stylised miniature 3D is not realistic. If YouTube thinks a scene might mislead, it may add the label itself.

**Concrete recommendation (do all of these):**

1. **Original scripts with a visible point of view.** Each script gets its own argument ("the auditor was the failure point", not a Wikipedia retelling). Never read news copy aloud; that is literally "reused content". Our script-writer agent plus `scriptcheck` already does this. Keep the human approval step and log it per film, for example a `review.md` with the approver, date and changes.
2. **No visual template reuse across films.** This is already our rule (miniature v3: no asset or scene reuse across films). It is now also our strongest YPP defence. Vary the act structure, the opening and the camera language per film. Don't run a fixed "cold open → 3 chapters → CTA" skeleton every time.
3. **Cadence: 1–2 long-form films per channel per week.** Daily AI uploads across both channels look like a "mass-produced" pattern. Fewer, better films also match what wins in 2026 (LEMMiNO, Axen).
4. **Evidence and credits on screen and in the description:** sources list, photo and document credits, a short "how this was made" line. This shows research and human curation.
5. **Shorts must not be raw slices of the long video.** Each Short gets its own hook line (through the script-writer) and its own framing or end beat. Near-duplicate Shorts across both channels are exactly the "minimal variation" pattern.
6. **Voice:** keep the one calm AI narrator. It is allowed. Never clone a real person's voice. Long term, the safest upgrade is a consistent, owned "channel voice" (our own licensed or cloned voice), so the channel is recognisably authored. Tick "altered or synthetic" only if a film uses photorealistic AI imagery of real events or people, which our style rules forbid anyway.
7. **Keep the two channels different in look details** (palette accent, typography, map style) so YouTube and viewers don't read them as one templated content farm.

## 6. Final recommendation

| | **Powder Keg** | **Partition Lines** |
|---|---|---|
| Niche | Corporate collapses and white-collar fraud ("how it blew up") | Geo-economics of borders, chokepoints and trade ("the lines that move money") |
| Name fit | A powder keg is pressure that explodes: companies, bubbles and frauds that blew up | Partition lines are borders and dividing lines on the map, and what crossing them costs |
| Planning RPM | **$4–12** (business $6–16 / finance $8–20 published; measured median far lower) | **$3–9** (education $3–7 published, $10.22 measured median; news $2.60 measured) |
| Saturation | Medium-high top, gaps in fresh 2026 cases, non-US cases, room reconstructions | Medium; gap in mechanism-first, non-presenter, map-to-miniature explainers |
| Viral driver | Record bankruptcy wave, steady fraud cases | Hormuz closure, tariffs, sanctions, rare earths, Arctic |
| Hero visual | The room, the document, the red founder | The map, the port, the red ship or line |
| Ad-safety | High (defamation is the risk) | Medium (avoid war imagery in thumbnails) |
| Style fit | 9/10 | 10/10 |

**Why this pair:**

1. **It is a real test.** The two differ in advertiser pool (finance/B2B vs education/general), audience (business and investing vs world-affairs and geography), dominant visual (interiors vs maps), and story arc (narrative rise-and-fall vs system explainer). If one wins, we learn which axis matters.
2. **Each name fits naturally,** so branding needs no rework.
3. **Both reuse our current topic radar** (scandals, big tech, China–US and money go to Powder Keg; geopolitics, China–US trade and sanctions go to Partition Lines). The radar only needs a channel filter, not new sources.
4. **Both avoid the weakest RPM bucket**, war and politics news, while keeping the drama.

**Alternative if the user wants maximum contrast:** Powder Keg = industrial and engineering disasters (#4). This is a lower RPM band ($4–9), a closer literal name fit, more sensitive-event ad risk, and less defamation risk.

## 8. Risks and unknowns (honest list)

1. **RPM data is weak.** The only Studio-measured dataset (AIR) has 3 channels for Business & Finance and 3 for News & Politics. The published "finance $20–50 CPM" tables give no samples or methods. Our real RPM could be half the planning band, especially if the audience skews India or Shorts. Measure in Studio after about 20 films before deciding.
2. **AIR measured Business & Finance median RPM ($2.01) below Education ($10.22).** If that holds, an explainer framing could out-earn "business" even on Partition Lines. The test will tell us.
3. **Saturation at the top of business documentaries is real.** MagnatesMedia, Coffeezilla and ColdFusion will often cover the same collapse. Our edge is the look and freshness, not exclusivity.
4. **Defamation risk (Powder Keg).** Name living people only with charges, filings or rulings. Use "alleged" plus sourcing. Never invent dialogue for real executives. Get a human review before publishing.
5. **Ad-suitability risk (Partition Lines).** The topics border on war. A Hormuz video can be tagged as a sensitive event or war and get limited ads. Keep imagery non-graphic and titles neutral, and check each upload's ad icon.
6. **News-cycle dependence.** The Hormuz-driven spike may fade. Partition Lines needs evergreen chokepoint and border films alongside topical ones.
7. **YPP inauthentic-content enforcement is opaque.** Review is partly automated and the policy text is vague ("looks like a template"). Two channels from one pipeline with one AI narrator are a visible pattern. Mitigate with per-film uniqueness, human review logs, distinct channel identities and a modest cadence. Even so, an initial YPP rejection is possible. Appeal with the process documented.
8. **Production speed vs news speed.** Our build time (hours per film) means we will usually be 3–10 days behind faster channels on breaking stories. Prefer the "explained properly" angle over being first.
9. **Unverified numbers in this report:**
   - Subscriber counts come from third-party trackers (vidIQ, Social Blade, SpeakRJ, SponsorRadar) and lag by weeks.
   - RealLifeLore, Wendover, Plainly Difficult and LegalEagle counts were not verified.
   - No verified 2025–26 breakout channel was found in business or geo-economics specifically. The breakout evidence is format-level (fern, LEMMiNO, Mack/Axen, WGOWS).
10. **No search-volume data.** Google Trends and YouTube search volume were not pulled (no API key, and Trends blocks scripted access). The "rising interest" claims rest on news supply and channel growth, not measured search curves.
11. **Many sources are vendor blogs** (OutlierKit, FluxNote, Autonolab, TaleTok) that sell tools. Figures from them are flagged in the tables, and YouTube's own Help pages were used for all policy statements.
