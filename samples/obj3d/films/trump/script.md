Framing: an evidence trial. Every exhibit gets a stamp: PROVEN (public record / official finding / plea), ALLEGED (claimed, not established),
DISPROVEN (contradicted by an official finding), UNVERIFIED (claim with no supporting evidence made public). The film draws no conclusion of
fact about whether anyone is an asset; allegations are always attributed. Mannequin figures only, no faces. Full fact sheet with quotes:
side-session notes trump-facts.md (checked 2026-11-14). approx. = rounded / estimate.

Sources (primary first):
- Mueller report, "Report on the Investigation into Russian Interference in the 2016 Presidential Election", Vol. I-II, released 18 Apr 2019, 448 pp. (justice.gov/archives/sco/file/1373816/dl).
  Quotes used: Russia interfered "in sweeping and systematic fashion"; the investigation "did not establish that members of the Trump Campaign conspired or coordinated with the Russian government";
  Vol. II: "while this report does not conclude that the President committed a crime, it also does not exonerate him"; Russia "perceived it would benefit from a Trump presidency" and the Campaign
  "expected it would benefit electorally from information stolen and released through Russian efforts"; Trump Moscow talks "as late as June 2016"; LOI with I.C. Expert (Andrei Rozov) Oct-Nov 2015;
  Cohen's Jan 2016 email to Peskov's office and ~20-min call with an assistant; Manafort polling data to Kilimnik, "could not reliably determine" why; Cohen never travelled to Prague;
  June 9 meeting: Veselnitskaya talked about the Magnitsky Act and adoptions; campaign-finance charge declined (willfulness, valuation). Scale: ~500 witnesses, 2,800+ subpoenas (approx.).
  Charges: 34 people and 3 companies; none for conspiring with Russia (CBS News / Time tallies).
- Indictments: 13 Russian nationals + 3 entities (IRA), 16 Feb 2018; 12 GRU officers, 13 Jul 2018 (DOJ).
- Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, bipartisan report Vol. 5 "Counterintelligence Threats and Vulnerabilities", 18 Aug 2020, 966 pp., Republican-led (Burr led the inquiry, Rubio acting chair at release).
  Kilimnik "a Russian intelligence officer"; Manafort's access and willingness to share information "represented a grave counterintelligence threat"; Veselnitskaya ties to the Russian government.
  Members drew opposite conclusions on collusion (Rubio statement; Democratic views) (Axios, RFE/RL Aug 2020).
- US Treasury press release, 15 Apr 2021: Kilimnik provided campaign polling data to Russian intelligence services (AP, Lawfare).
- Manafort: campaign from Mar 2016, chairman 19 May - 19 Aug 2016; convicted on 8 counts of financial fraud Aug 2018; pardoned Dec 2020.
- Cohen pleaded guilty 29 Nov 2018 to lying to Congress about the Moscow project timeline (Reuters / Al Jazeera).
- 1987 Moscow trip at Intourist's invitation; 1996 announced Moscow project (~$250M, never built) (Washington Post via ADN, 17 Jan 2017; Moscow Times archive 1996). Bayrock / Felix Sater Moscow attempt mid-2000s.
- Don Jr. 2008: "Russians make up a pretty disproportionate cross-section of a lot of our assets" (real-estate conference, Sept 2008, reported by eTurboNews; no recording) -> "reportedly".
- Palm Beach mansion: bought 2004 ~$41.35M, sold 16 Jul 2008 for $95M to Dmitry Rybolovlev (Inman, 2018).
- Miss Universe 9 Nov 2013, Crocus City Hall (Aras Agalarov); Emin Agalarov performed; Trump's June 2013 tweet asking if Putin would come and become his "new best friend";
  no Putin meeting (Peskov, May 2016; press) - Mueller makes no explicit finding on it.
- June 2016 meeting: Goldstone email 3 Jun 2016 ("part of Russia and its government's support for Mr. Trump"); Don Jr.: "If it's what you say I love it"; meeting 9 Jun 2016, Trump Tower (Mueller; NBC).
- Steele dossier: Christopher Steele for Fusion GPS, funded via Perkins Coie by the Clinton campaign and DNC (FEC fine, Axios 30 Mar 2022); published by BuzzFeed 10 Jan 2017.
  DOJ IG Horowitz, 9 Dec 2019: 17 significant errors or omissions in the Carter Page FISA applications; no documentary evidence of political bias in opening the investigation; the investigation
  (Crossfire Hurricane) opened 31 Jul 2016 on the Papadopoulos tip from an Australian diplomat. Primary sub-source (Danchenko) described info as "word of mouth and hearsay"; acquitted 18 Oct 2022 of lying to the FBI.
  Durham report, 15 May 2023: investigation opened on "raw, unanalyzed, and uncorroborated intelligence" (IG disagreed on the threshold).
- Helsinki, 16 Jul 2018: ~2 h private meeting with interpreters only (approx.); "I don't see any reason why it would be"; corrected to "wouldn't" 17 Jul; McCain: "one of the most disgraceful performances
  by an American president in memory" (NPR, CNBC).
- Counter-evidence: 60 Russian officials expelled 26 Mar 2018 (NPR); Javelin sale to Ukraine approved 1 Mar 2018, 210 missiles (approx. $47M) (RFE/RL); Rosneft + Lukoil sanctions 22 Oct 2025 (Treasury).
- 2025: Alaska summit 15 Aug 2025 (Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson) (AP).
- Later claims, UNVERIFIED: Yuri Shvets (ex-KGB major) via Craig Unger's book, Guardian Jan 2021 ("cultivated as an asset for 40 years") (Snopes); Alnur Mussayev (ex-Kazakh security official) Facebook post
  Feb 2025, "Krasnov" codename, 1987 - no evidence offered, fact-checkers found none (Euronews 13 Mar 2025). No official response to Mussayev found -> the film does not claim a denial.
- FBI opened a counterintelligence inquiry into whether Trump was working for Russia after Comey's firing (9 May 2017) (NYT, 11 Jan 2019); Mueller appointed 17 May 2017. Trump: "I never worked for Russia" (VOA, 14 Jan 2019).
- No US government body has publicly concluded that Trump is or was a Russian asset.

#ch1 the question
NARR (quiet, serious): Washington, early morning. One question has followed one man for almost ten years.
NARR (measured): Is Donald Trump a Russian asset? Someone Moscow recruited, or at least someone it can lean on?
NARR (dry): Some people are sure he is. Others are sure the whole thing was a hoax.
NARR (explaining): This film is neither. Think of it as a trial, where the only evidence allowed is the public record.
NARR (explaining): Every exhibit gets one of four stamps. Proven. Alleged. Disproven. Or unverified.
NARR (matter-of-fact): There's a lot of record. Special Counsel Robert Mueller's report alone is four hundred and forty-eight pages.
#ch2 exhibit a, the moscow business
NARR (storytelling): Exhibit A starts in nineteen eighty-seven, in the last years of the Soviet Union.
NARR (storytelling): A New York developer flies to Moscow, invited by the Soviet state travel agency, Intourist.
NARR (explaining): He looks at sites for a luxury hotel near Red Square. Nothing gets built.
NARR (explaining): In nineteen ninety-six he's back, announcing a two hundred and fifty million dollar Moscow tower. Nothing again.
NARR (explaining): Later, a partner firm with a Russian-born developer, Felix Sater, chases a Trump-branded Moscow deal too.
NARR (matter-of-fact): In two thousand eight, his son Don Junior reportedly told a real estate conference that Russians made up a pretty disproportionate cross-section of a lot of their assets.
NARR (explaining): That same year, a Florida mansion Trump had bought for about forty-one million dollars sold to a Russian billionaire for ninety-five million.
NARR (dry): Stamp check. Russian buyers and Russian partners: proven. A Trump tower in Moscow: never built.
NARR (measured): Business with Russians isn't a crime, or spying. But it explains why Moscow keeps coming back.
#ch3 exhibit b, miss universe
NARR (storytelling): Exhibit B. November twenty thirteen. The Miss Universe pageant, which Trump co-owned, comes to Moscow.
NARR (explaining): The venue is Crocus City Hall, built by billionaire developer Aras Agalarov. His son Emin, a pop singer, performs.
NARR (dry): Months earlier, Trump had tweeted, asking whether Putin would come, and whether he'd become his new best friend.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Putin didn't come. The Kremlin later said the two never met that weekend.
NARR (explaining): But the trip made friends. The Agalarovs and Trump talked about building a tower in Moscow together.
NARR (dry): Remember the Agalarovs. Their publicist writes an email in twenty sixteen that changes everything.
#ch4 exhibit c, trump tower moscow
NARR (serious): Exhibit C is the deal that stayed alive during the campaign.
NARR (matter-of-fact): June twenty fifteen. Trump announces he's running for president.
NARR (explaining): That autumn, the Trump Organization signs a letter of intent with a Russian developer, for a Trump Tower Moscow.
NARR (explaining): His lawyer, Michael Cohen, runs the project. Felix Sater is back as the go-between.
NARR (explaining): In January twenty sixteen, Cohen emails the office of Putin's spokesman, Dmitry Peskov, asking for help with the project.
NARR (dry): On the campaign trail, the candidate kept saying he had nothing to do with Russia.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Mueller found the Moscow talks went on as late as June twenty sixteen. By then, Trump was the presumptive Republican nominee.
NARR (serious): In November twenty eighteen, Cohen pleaded guilty to lying to Congress about exactly that timeline.
NARR (dry): Stamp check. Moscow talks during the campaign: proven. Cohen lied about them: proven, by his own guilty plea.
#ch5 exhibit d, the meeting
NARR (storytelling): Exhibit D. June third, twenty sixteen. An email lands for Donald Trump Junior.
NARR (explaining): It's from Rob Goldstone, the Agalarovs' publicist. He's offering documents that would incriminate Hillary Clinton.
NARR (slow, precise): He calls it part of Russia and its government's support for Mr. Trump.
NARR (dry): Trump Junior replies: if it's what you say, I love it.
NARR (matter-of-fact): June ninth. Trump Tower, New York.
NARR (explaining): Trump Junior, son-in-law Jared Kushner and campaign chairman Paul Manafort sit down with a Russian lawyer, Natalia Veselnitskaya.
NARR (explaining): She talks mostly about a US sanctions law and adoptions. No dirt changes hands.
NARR (explaining): Mueller weighed a campaign finance charge, and declined. He couldn't prove they knew it was illegal, or value the promised dirt.
NARR (matter-of-fact): The Senate committee later found the lawyer had significant ties to the Russian government.
NARR (dry): Stamp check. Willing to take help from Moscow: in their own emails. A crime: never charged.
#ch6 exhibit e, the polling data
NARR (serious): Exhibit E is the one investigators found hardest to explain.
NARR (explaining): Paul Manafort chaired the campaign that summer. For years before that, he'd worked for pro-Russian politicians in Ukraine.
NARR (storytelling): On August second, twenty sixteen, he meets his long-time associate from Ukraine, Konstantin Kilimnik, at a cigar club in Manhattan.
NARR (explaining): Manafort had been passing Kilimnik the campaign's internal polling data.
NARR (dry): Mueller could not reliably determine why, or what happened to it next.
NARR (serious): The bipartisan Senate committee went further. It called Kilimnik a Russian intelligence officer.
NARR (serious): And it called Manafort's willingness to share information a grave counterintelligence threat.
NARR (matter-of-fact): In twenty twenty-one, the US Treasury said Kilimnik passed that polling data to Russian intelligence services.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Manafort was later convicted of fraud over his Ukraine money, and pardoned by Trump in twenty twenty.
NARR (dry): Stamp check. Polling data handed to a man the Senate calls a Russian intelligence officer: proven. Why: still unknown.
#ch7 exhibit f, the dossier
NARR (dry): Exhibit F is the most famous, and the weakest.
NARR (explaining): In twenty sixteen, a former British spy, Christopher Steele, wrote a series of memos about Trump and Russia.
NARR (explaining): The research was paid for, through a law firm, by the Clinton campaign and the Democratic Party.
NARR (matter-of-fact): BuzzFeed published the memos in January twenty seventeen. Among the lurid claims: a compromising tape.
NARR (dry): Steele wrote that Michael Cohen met Russian officials in Prague. Mueller found Cohen never went to Prague.
NARR (matter-of-fact): The tape was never corroborated.
NARR (explaining): In twenty nineteen, the Justice Department's inspector general found seventeen significant errors and omissions in FBI surveillance requests that leaned on the dossier.
NARR (dry): Steele's main source told the FBI that much of it was word of mouth and hearsay.
NARR (explaining): The inspector general found no political bias in opening the investigation, which began with a tip from an Australian diplomat.
NARR (explaining): In twenty twenty-three, Special Counsel John Durham disagreed: the FBI had acted on raw, uncorroborated intelligence.
NARR (dry): Stamp check. The dossier's headline claims: unverified, and in places disproven. But the rest of the case doesn't stand or fall with it.
#ch8 the official verdicts
NARR (matter-of-fact): First, Russia. Mueller wrote that the Russian government interfered in the twenty sixteen election in sweeping and systematic fashion.
NARR (explaining): A troll factory in Saint Petersburg ran fake American accounts. Military intelligence hacked Democratic emails and leaked them.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Thirteen Russians from the troll factory and twelve military intelligence officers were indicted. Interference: proven.
NARR (explaining): Mueller also found Russia believed it would benefit from a Trump presidency, and the campaign expected to benefit from what Russia stole and released.
NARR (measured): But on the central charge, the investigation did not establish that the campaign conspired or coordinated with the Russian government.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Thirty-four people were charged in the inquiry. None for conspiring with Russia.
NARR (measured): Not enough evidence to charge a conspiracy. That is not proof there was none. And it is not proof there was one.
NARR (slow, precise): On obstruction, Mueller wrote that the report does not conclude the President committed a crime, but it also does not exonerate him.
NARR (explaining): In twenty twenty, the Republican-led Senate committee published its final volume: nine hundred and sixty-six pages.
NARR (dry): Republicans on the committee said it showed no collusion. Democrats said it showed exactly that. Same facts, two readings.
#ch9 exhibit g, helsinki and the defence
NARR (storytelling): Exhibit G. Helsinki, July twenty eighteen.
NARR (explaining): Trump and Putin meet for about two hours, with only their interpreters in the room.
NARR (explaining): At the press conference, asked about Russia's interference, Trump says: I don't see any reason why it would be.
NARR (dry): The next day, he says he meant wouldn't.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Republican senator John McCain called it one of the most disgraceful performances by an American president in memory.
NARR (explaining): But the defence has exhibits too. In twenty eighteen, the administration expelled sixty Russian officials after a nerve agent attack in Britain.
NARR (explaining): That March, it approved selling Javelin anti-tank missiles to Ukraine, which the previous administration had refused to do.
NARR (matter-of-fact): In his second term, he met Putin again, in Alaska, in August twenty twenty-five. Then in October, he sanctioned Russia's two biggest oil companies.
NARR (dry): Stamp check. Strange deference in public: on the record. Russia policy: mixed.
#ch10 the unverified claims
NARR (explaining): In twenty twenty-one, a former KGB major, Yuri Shvets, claimed the KGB had cultivated Trump as an asset for forty years.
NARR (explaining): In twenty twenty-five, a former Kazakh security official claimed on Facebook that Trump was recruited in nineteen eighty-seven, under the codename Krasnov.
NARR (dry): Neither offered documents. Fact checkers found no evidence. Stamp: unverified.
NARR (matter-of-fact): After Comey's firing, the FBI did open an inquiry into whether Trump worked for Russia. He has said he never did.
NARR (measured): No US agency has ever publicly concluded that he did.
#ch11 the gavel
NARR (explaining): Proven: Russia interfered. Moscow talks ran into the campaign. The campaign took the meeting. Manafort shared the polling data.
NARR (explaining): Disproven or unverified: the dossier's big claims, and the recruitment stories.
NARR (measured): Not established, after years of investigation: a criminal conspiracy, or public evidence that Trump takes orders from Moscow.
NARR (quiet): A Russia that expected to benefit. A campaign willing to listen. And no proof of a handler.
NARR (quiet, final): The gavel is yours.
