#ch1 cold open
NARR (quiet, serious) @1.2: In Santa Clara, California, the lights are still on at the headquarters of the world's most valuable chip company.
NARR (matter-of-fact): This week, the man who started it thirty three years ago walked out of the building for good.
NARR (explaining): Jensen Huang, Nvidia's founder, resigned as chief executive and left the board.
NARR (dry): His last week in charge involved an affair, an alleged Chinese spy, and a stock that dropped as much as thirteen percent in one day.
NARR (serious): And a question that reaches Washington. Did the world's most wanted chips end up where they were not allowed to go?
NARR (curious): So how did the company at the center of the AI boom lose its founder in five days? And what is actually proven?
#ch2 how nvidia got here
NARR (storytelling): Nineteen ninety-three. Three engineers meet at a Denny's diner in East San Jose and start a company to make graphics chips.
NARR (light): One of them is Jensen Huang, born in Taiwan, who came to America as a boy of about nine.
NARR (explaining): In nineteen ninety-nine Nvidia sells the GeForce two fifty-six, and markets it as the world's first GPU.
NARR (explaining): A GPU does thousands of small sums at the same time. Perfect for drawing video game worlds.
NARR (explaining): In two thousand six, Nvidia releases CUDA, software that lets scientists use those chips for any kind of maths.
NARR (building): Then in twenty twelve, a neural network called AlexNet wins a famous image contest, trained on two Nvidia gaming cards.
NARR (explaining): Researchers notice. Teaching an AI is mostly the same small sums, repeated billions of times.
NARR (building): When ChatGPT arrives in late twenty twenty-two, every tech giant suddenly wants Nvidia chips, by the warehouse.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Analysts estimate Nvidia sells roughly eighty to ninety percent of the chips used to train AI.
NARR (explaining): Its data centers division alone brought in about a hundred and ninety four billion dollars last fiscal year.
NARR (counting): The market value goes from one trillion dollars in twenty twenty-three, to four trillion, to five trillion.
NARR (dry): A graphics card company from a diner booth, now worth more than most countries produce in a year.
#ch3 why chips to china matter
NARR (explaining): Which is exactly why Washington cares where those chips go.
NARR (serious): The US government says advanced AI chips can help a military design weapons, crack codes, and run mass surveillance.
NARR (explaining): So in October twenty twenty-two, it banned exports of the most powerful AI chips to China.
NARR (dry): Nvidia answered with slightly slower versions built to fit just under the line.
NARR (dry): In twenty twenty-three, Washington moved the line. Nvidia designed another chip, called the H twenty, to fit under it again.
NARR (explaining): In April twenty twenty-five, even the H twenty needed a licence. Nvidia warned of up to five and a half billion dollars in charges.
NARR (dry): Then a deal. Sales could resume, if the US government got fifteen percent of the revenue.
NARR (matter-of-fact): By December, a stronger chip, the H two hundred, was allowed with a twenty five percent cut. The newest Blackwell chips stayed banned.
NARR (serious): And where rules block a sale, smugglers look for a route. Through middlemen in Singapore and Malaysia.
NARR (explaining): In twenty twenty-five, Singapore charged three men over server deals worth about three hundred and ninety million US dollars.
NARR (explaining): The same year, US prosecutors charged two people with shipping top Nvidia chips to China.
NARR (dry): So a chip ban is only as strong as the paperwork in the middle. Remember Singapore.
#ch4 alice huang and megaspeed
NARR (storytelling): Which brings us to Alice Huang. No relation to Jensen. She is a Chinese citizen, and a tech executive.
NARR (explaining): She was managing director of Megaspeed, a data center company based in Singapore.
NARR (explaining): According to the research group C4ADS, cited by Reuters, she was also its controlling shareholder.
NARR (matter-of-fact): In October twenty twenty-three, Megaspeed became an official Nvidia cloud partner.
NARR (explaining): Cloud partners buy Nvidia's hardware and rent its computing power out to other customers.
NARR (explaining): Over two to three years, Megaspeed and a Malaysian affiliate bought about four point eight billion dollars of Nvidia hardware.
NARR (serious): US officials suspected some of it might end up in China. The Commerce Department reportedly opened a probe.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Megaspeed denies diverting anything. It says inspections, including by Nvidia, found no violations.
NARR (explaining): Singapore ran its own probe, seized her passport, then found too little evidence to charge her. She left, and is barred from returning.
NARR (matter-of-fact): Singapore said American help with its investigation was limited.
NARR (storytelling): Reuters verified photos of Jensen and Alice Huang together going back to December twenty twenty-three.
NARR (dry): A chip boss, and a big customer's executive in a market under export watch. Few people outside knew what the relationship was.
