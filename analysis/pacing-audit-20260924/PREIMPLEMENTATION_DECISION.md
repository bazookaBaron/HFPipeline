## What the references actually do

The common unit is a **semantic visual event**: an entity enters, changes status, connects, moves, is contradicted, or yields a result at a relevant spoken word. The references do *not* share one cutting pattern. R2 keeps its router/cards visual carrier for about 125 seconds and mutates it repeatedly; R3 has 32 short numbered visual scaffolds and 31 cuts; R4 repeatedly redraws a related diagram with ~150 ms crossfades; R5 has 33 scaffolds but 63 internal sub-states. R1 returns to a road/traffic visual family many times. Counting mounted scenes as beats would conflate these distinct mechanisms.

The [sentence atlas](SCRIPT_VISUAL_ATLAS.md#storyboard-grammar-and-comparison-with-ours) gives the complete script-to-picture mapping. Its pattern is: establish a concrete system, let each spoken noun or causal verb change that system, use persistent color to track entities, and show consequence rather than restating narration as a card. The [graphic atlas](MOTION_GRAPHICS_ATLAS.md#repeated-signatures-closest-matches-and-gaps) details actual repeated operations: status swaps, one-frame in-place corrections, line/connector draws, routed packets, typing/cursor progress, counters, semantic color/state, and continued content within a stable shell. Several are not expressible by merely shortening our existing clips.

The first three seconds and last five seconds have their own observations in the sentence atlas and original logs. In brief, each reference opens with the system/object and a changing state within the opening seconds; our render begins with an empty card, reveals its title at ~0.67 s, then mostly holds it until ~5.5 s. The references finish with a visual consequence or resolved state near the spoken conclusion; ours has a near-empty mailroom-analogy interval ~62.60–65.63 s and a static takeaway to 70.2 s. These are comparisons of the supplied outputs, not claims that every future video must use an identical opening or ending template.

Our render demonstrates the failure more clearly than an average beat rate: linked-list nodes arrive ~21.67–22.50 s, but the named concept is spoken around 25.86 s; O(N) appears ~35.97 s, several seconds before the spoken term; a gauge reaches 100% ~51.30 s and then remains pixel-identical for ~3.83 s. The mailroom analogy has no logged mailroom visual. Some references deliberately hold a scaffold for longer, but useful internal state or process motion continues. The beat detector's reference range (~51–80/min versus ours ~32/min) is therefore a diagnostic, **not** an invariant acceptance threshold for every style.

## Correction to `MOTION_PACING_PLAN.md`

The existing plan's architecture verdict—**do not start a new project**—is supported. Its proposed general rule of 25–40 independently mounted scenes is not. A narration sentence, a persistent visual host, a graphic state, an animated event, and a hard cut are separate concepts. In the current `composeHtml.js`, each scene is a fresh component mount with its own props and timeline; `scaffoldId` plus `enter:"continue"` cannot by itself transfer prior DOM/state. Likewise, a universal nested `props.items[i].at` path is not safe across the catalog because several list props are encoded differently, and the current `_at` restriction guards against writing timing numbers into content props. These are contract-level changes, not tuning constants.

Do not set a universal 3–5-second scaffold lifetime, automatically triple scene count, or require perpetual movement to eliminate every long hold. Do not assume a visual-density detector understands whether a beat adds meaning. Use style-aware diagnostics and semantic checks. Camera moves, elaborate transitions, and more saturated color are not first-order deficits in these five references. Our current on-screen entrance durations are already near the observed range after the 1.8× speed-up; retime only after the audio policy is measured.

## Architecture verdict

Keep the repository, HyperFrames/GSAP rendering substrate, component registry, scripting/repair loop, F5-TTS + word alignment, and control panel. Build a **versioned v2 authoring/timing contract** next to v1. It should describe:

1. `story/chapter`: claims, hook, mechanism, consequence, resolution, and factual constraints.
2. `narration`: continuous final-output-time word IDs, audio offsets, prosody/pause policy.
3. `visualHost`: persistent scaffold/layout, entity identity, initial complete state, duration and replacement style. A host may span many sentences; multiple hosts may occupy one chapter.
4. `event`: deterministic timestamp/word reference, host/entity target, typed operation (reveal/update/highlight/strike/connect/route/count/type/remove), value, animation preset, optional sound cue, and state after the operation.
5. `styleProfile`: an observed visual grammar (e.g., dark persistent system, light editorial, cut-heavy numbered steps), semantic palette, caption/character rules, and legal asset provenance.

The renderer must be seek-safe: a random seek at time *t* reconstructs the same host state whether or not the player previously visited earlier frames. Either mount one host for its full lifetime and run its deterministic event track, or explicitly materialize a complete state snapshot at every new mount. The latter is a valid alternative only if transition snapshots prove equivalence; a label named `continue` is insufficient. Keep the old contract available for rollback until v2 passes end-to-end pilots.

## Ordered work plan, with evidence gates

1. **Freeze and validate the baseline.** Preserve the six videos, source logs, ASR, render configuration, and current repo state. Calibrate the beat/empty-frame detector against the manually logged references, excluding caption/mascot-only changes. Report its error, not just its score. Add stage-level timing instrumentation to one baseline rerun; the persisted job gives 767 s total for ten scenes, not a reliable per-stage cost split.
2. **Specify the v2 contract and one-host prototype.** Build a single real chapter on `deployment-dashboard` or `node-graph-diagram`: several spoken claims, one visual host, multiple state changes and word-anchored item events. Prove first-frame content, continuity at every event boundary, deterministic random seeks, and no content-type corruption. This is the go/no-go proof that the existing renderer can support the required grammar without a rewrite.
3. **Measure narration before changing default speed.** A/B the actual voice at a few F5 speeds against the current 1.8× post-speed result. Record delivered WPM, clipped/unnatural speech, leading/trailing silence, and word alignment error. Shift timestamps and audio together when trimming silence; preserve intentional breaths. Benchmark a scene, a several-sentence chunk, and a longer chapter before choosing TTS chunk size; continuous narration is a desired interface, not proof that one model call is faster or stable.
4. **Implement typed operations through a small component set.** Registry metadata declares supported operations/item identities; adapters handle JSON arrays and pipe-delimited items explicitly. Resolve anchors by stable word ID or occurrence-aware phrase, validate target/bounds/order, and never overwrite content props with numeric times. Start with graph, deployment/status, terminal, list/step, browser/window, metric/counter, and connector/packet operations. Expand only when a pilot topic requires another graphic family. The [graphic atlas](MOTION_GRAPHICS_ATLAS.md) is the worklist, not a mandate to clone every reference image.
5. **Fix the click-to-local-video contract.** Treat the user's requested length as *final MP4 seconds*; remove the hidden 45→90-second floor or make duration negotiation explicit. Pass one validated configuration through UI → queue → script → voice → render, including output speed, theme, captions, sound, handle, and character choice. Give every visual host content on its first visible frame. Persist stage and failure state so unattended jobs can recover or fail visibly after a restart. Scheduler and social publishing are separate scope from one-click local output.
6. **Build layered, bounded quality assurance.** Pre-voice: factual/storyboard checks, visual state graph reachability, asset provenance. Pre-render: word/event alignment, duration budget, initial host states, deterministic ordering. Render: HyperFrames checks, transition and random-seek snapshots, contrast/layout bounds. Post-render: calibrated semantic-content gaps, silence/music/SFX checks, caption timing, and selected frame comparisons. Classify a failure as script, timing, asset, component, renderer, or style, then repair only that layer with bounded retries; do not automatically regenerate the whole script for a component defect.
7. **Pilot on three unlike topics.** Use the supplied hash-collision topic plus a persistent-diagram subject and a cut-heavy procedural subject. Require final duration near request, no empty content on host entry, speech/event timing within a measured tolerance, seek/transition equivalence, no technical check errors, and human judgment of factual correctness, narration, sound, and visual coherence. Compare each to the appropriate reference *style*, not the same scene-count target. Only after these pass, make v2 the default topic-button path.

## Production feasibility and unanswered decisions

The observed 767-second job is evidence of current latency for **one** topic, not a per-video target or evidence that 3× scenes costs 3× as much. Current script retries, sequential voice calls, one-GPU queue, rendering and speed-up all contribute; instrument them before estimating throughput and cost. Reusing the stack is a lower-risk route than recreating the 64-component catalog and control flow, but the v2 contract plus durable QA is a substantial refactor.

Do not copy reference mascots, logos, still photographs, voices, or music without provenance/rights. Our rendered SpongeBob sticker should be replaced by an original, owned/licensed character before autonomous distribution. Exact reference asset rights are unknown. Publishing is not part of the requested local-video outcome and would require explicit platform/credential decisions.

The [audio/assets/feasibility audit](AUDIO_ASSETS_FEASIBILITY.md) gives the complete six-video measurements. Its strongest result is that 16.7% of our extracted audio's 20 ms windows fall below −60 dBFS, compared with 0.1–0.2% for each reference. Our render schedules 25 SFX cues and no music track; this establishes placement, **not** audible effectiveness. Limited mixed-audio pitch screening put our median near 134.5 Hz and the references near 205–229 Hz, but harmonics/background and the smaller accepted sample prevent a reliable perceived-energy or voice-quality conclusion. The current asset inventory includes the embedded-raster SpongeBob SVG, one repo-synthesized whoosh, and five MP3 cues without recorded source/license provenance. Exact reference music, audible SFX density, voice-clone suitability, and monetary cost remain unverified. Resolve those with actual listening, source/provenance records, and measured runs; do not infer them from waveform floor or pitch alone.
