# "Thirty percent, then dead" (campfire, Leo + Walter, ~50 s final, voice at 1.3x)

Premise: Leo's phone said thirty percent and then died on the snowy hill, and he cannot let it go. Walter, his grandfather, walks him through cold batteries in plain words. Both stay seated by the fire the whole time. The phone and the battery exist only in words and in the MG.
Payload: inside a phone battery, tiny lithium ions swim through a liquid between two sides; cold makes that liquid thick (like honey), so the ions move slowly; the phone asks for power, the slow ions cannot deliver it fast enough, the voltage sags below the phone's safety line and it shuts off, with charge still inside; warm it and the charge is usable again.
Human question: "Did I lose my battery?" Turn: the thirty percent never went anywhere, it was stuck. Landing: not broken, just slow, like you before breakfast.
Running line: "thirty percent" (L1, L9, L13, L14) and "honey"/"slow" (L6-L10, L18).

LEO (shocked) [CU]: Grandpa, my phone said thirty percent. Then it died. Out in the snow.
WALTER (amused) [MS]: It wasn't dead. It was cold.
LEO (curious) [OTS]: Cold and empty are different?
WALTER (matter-of-fact) [MS]: Very. Inside the battery is a liquid, and tiny lithium bits swim through it. That swimming is your power.
LEO (thinking) [OTS]: Swimming. Okay.
WALTER (pleased) [MS]: Now freeze it. The liquid turns thick, like honey.
LEO (surprised) [OTS]: Honey?
WALTER (amused) [MS]: They still swim. Just slowly. The phone wants power now, and they can't get there in time.
LEO (confused) [OTS]: So where did the thirty percent go?
WALTER (matter-of-fact) [MS]: Nowhere. It's stuck in the honey.
LEO (curious) [OTS]: Then why does the phone quit?
WALTER (firm, light) [MS]: Slow swimmers push weakly. The voltage sags. Under a certain line, the phone shuts itself off to stay safe.
LEO (shocked) [CU]: With thirty percent still in there?
WALTER (warm) [2S]: Warm it up, the honey thins, and the thirty percent comes back.
LEO (curious) [OTS]: Warm it how?
WALTER (matter-of-fact) [MS]: Inside your coat. Close to you.
LEO (grinning) [OTS]: So the battery isn't broken.
WALTER (dry, flat) [CU]: Just slow. Like you before breakfast.

#end

Delivery notes (voice): normal conversational pace (the track is sped up 1.3x after, pitch kept). Leo: shocked, curious, surprised, playful; L13 is the turn, a grin on L17. Walter: warm, smiling in the voice, falls to a low finish; only the last line is dry and flat, after a beat.
Seated: no actions at all. Nobody takes out a phone or warms anything; the phone, the battery and the ions are words + MG only.

<!-- Sources (plain terms)
- A lithium-ion cell moves lithium ions through a liquid electrolyte between the anode and the cathode while the cell discharges (the electrons go round the outside circuit): Battery University BU-204 "How do Lithium Batteries Work?"; Goodenough and Park, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 135 (2013) 1167.
- In the cold the electrolyte gets more viscous and its ionic conductivity falls, the charge-transfer reaction at the electrodes slows, and the cell's internal resistance rises: Ji, Zhang, Wang, "Li-ion cells operating at low temperature", Electrochimica Acta 2006 (and the review Zhu et al. 2019); Battery University BU-502 "Discharging at High and Low Temperatures".
- With higher internal resistance the terminal voltage under load drops (V = open-circuit voltage - I x R). A phone shuts off when the voltage crosses its low-voltage cutoff, even though the cell still holds charge; the charge is usable again after warming: BU-502 and BU-808; Apple Support "About batteries and cold temperatures"
- Apple lists 0-35 C as the operating ambient range for iPhone and says a phone may shut down in the cold, and it works again once warmed: Apple Support "Operating temperature for iPhone".
- "Honey" is a loose picture for a thicker (more viscous) electrolyte, not a measured ratio.
- Warm in your coat: body warmth and the heat the battery makes while it discharges help (BU-502); the numbers are illustrative.
